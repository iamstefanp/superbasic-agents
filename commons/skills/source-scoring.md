# Source Scoring

Every source carries a tier. A source without one is a claim about a claim.
Carried by: Researcher, Reviewer, Strategist.

**GOLD** — confirmed, triangulated across two or more *independent* sources.
Independence is the whole test: three articles tracing back to one press
release are one source wearing three hats, and that is the most common
false-Gold there is.

**SILVER** — probable. A single credible source, cross-referenced against
context and not contradicted. Most good research is Silver, honestly labelled.

**BRONZE** — inference, partial evidence, a source you could not fully
verify, or something answered from memory. Bronze is legitimate. Bronze
presented as Gold is not.

---

**How to tier, in practice:**

Ask where the claim originated, not where you read it. Follow it back as far
as it goes. If two sources both trace to the same origin, you have one
source. If you cannot tell, that uncertainty is itself the tier: Bronze.

**Fetched beats remembered.** A page you actually read is evidence. A page
you recall reading is Bronze at best — say so.

**Failed fetches are reported, not silently replaced.** If a source would not
load, say it would not load. Substituting a different source and presenting
it as the intended one is how a pool quietly rots.

**Date every source.** A Gold source about a fast-moving subject can be
stale enough to be wrong. Tier answers "how well established"; date answers
"how still true."
